# Broader generated-speech preferences and speaker conditioning

This follows two verified failures: target-only component weight transfer
regressed custom pronunciation, and reference reconstruction lowered fitting
loss without improving generated voice/pronunciation. The previous text-only
DPO pilot had just 22 fitting cases and did not generalize. Its result does not
settle whether generated-speech preferences work with broader coverage and a
different adaptation variable.

## Collection registered November 11, 2026

The source policy is the frozen full epoch-2 FP32 LoRA model, keeping the
original speaker encoder and codec. Collect four generated candidates for each
of two hash-selected texts per original training speaker. Select 64 speakers,
holding the first 16 out of future preference fitting. Remove fitting cases
whose target/reference transcript overlaps preference validation.

The resulting registration contains **95 fitting cases from 48 speakers** and
**32 validation cases from 16 speakers**: **508 generated recordings**. One
fitting case was removed for transcript overlap. These are original supervised
training speakers; preference validation is separate from adapter fitting,
not unseen by the original acoustic model. Development/custom targets are not
preference-training examples.

Four candidates share one batch seed per case and have explicit draw indices.
This new training-collection backend is CUDA FP32 SDPA, with TF32 disabled and
CPU codec decoding. It is not claimed to reproduce four separately seeded
generations. The established CPU development/custom backend and both fixed
seeds remain unchanged. A real first batch produced four natural-EOS outputs
in 17.28 seconds with peak GPU allocation 3,967,705,600 bytes. That single batch
is a feasibility observation, not a corpus-wide speed benchmark.

Actual sampled 16-codebook trajectories are saved alongside audio. The first
codebook is checked against generation tokens up to each sample's first EOS;
batch padding is excluded, and truncated generations cannot become fabricated
EOS preference examples. Every sample receives pinned ASR CER/WER and cosine
against the original reference. Candidate pairs must improve at least one
objective and be no worse on either; voice-only pairs require cosine gain
at least 0.001. All candidates remain in the report, including truncated ones.

This uses the joint candidate-filtering principle studied by
[Koel-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05236v2). Its architecture and scale differ,
so neither its hyperparameters nor its reported gains are assumed to transfer.

Collection and scoring completed with **508 recordings, no truncations, and
468 valid pairs**. The independent audit retained all 95 fitting cases across
48 speakers (352 pairs, 187 improving pronunciation) and all 32 validation
cases across 16 speakers (116 pairs, 65 improving pronunciation). Audio and
trajectory hashes, sampled EOS, transcript tokens, reference codecs, edit
counts and pair construction passed. Re-extracted voice cosines differed by
at most 5.37e-7. These are training-data checks, not development/custom results.

Across every collected candidate, fitting-partition CER/WER was 6.03%/13.30%
with speaker-macro cosine 0.978845; validation-partition CER/WER was
4.34%/10.33% with cosine 0.980512. These newly sampled training-policy outputs
are not directly comparable to the fixed CPU evaluation panel.

## New adaptation variable

`training/speaker_preference_adapter.py` supplies a shared rank-16 speaker-input
adapter: 32,768 FP32 parameters, with all acoustic weights frozen. Its initial
output is exactly zero. A smooth norm constraint keeps each speaker-vector
correction below 5% of that vector's norm. It uses the reference embedding only,
not the target's audio or an utterance-specific optimized parameter.

The fitting objective will use generated chosen/rejected trajectories and
their complete autoregressive log probabilities, including all residual
codebooks and natural EOS. It does not optimize reference reconstruction CE.
The same conditioning hook is used by generation and full-ICL teacher forcing.
The established sequence module remains unchanged; the new module restores
the speaker-input gradient explicitly at its native prefix position.

Tests cover no-op initialization, the norm constraint, exact agreement with
native full-ICL prefix construction and equality of replayed DPO gradients to
the full two-trajectory computation. Frozen acoustic parameters receive no
gradients. These are implementation checks, not speech-quality evidence.

The full 0.6B model also passed a real-data smoke check: the zero adapter
reproduced the frozen full-model CPU control waveform byte-for-byte, its
initial trajectory log probabilities matched the unmodified path, and a
generated-pair DPO backward pass produced an up-matrix gradient norm of
0.180069 with zero down-matrix gradient as expected at zero-up initialization.
No optimizer step was taken; acoustic hashes remained unchanged. All 83
training tests passed. The arbitrary pair order used for this numerical check
is not a preference-quality claim.

Speaker-input optimization is also motivated by
[ASRRL-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05421v1), but this shared low-rank preference
adapter is not a reproduction of its PPO policy. The previous failed
reference-loss experiment likewise did not test its generated-speech rewards.

## Before fitting or promotion

First finish and audit the broader collection. Check pair coverage by speaker,
target, pronunciation improvement and voice-only improvement. Use speaker-
balanced fitting so the one removed case does not underweight its speaker.
Register optimizer/checkpoint settings before fitting and freeze reference
likelihoods at the initial full-LoRA policy. Every resulting checkpoint must
receive generated CER/WER and voice evaluation, including an unadapted control.

## Frozen fitting settings

The fitting/evaluation driver is `training/speaker_preference_training.py`.
Its source, dependencies, runtime and settings were registered in the external
`speaker-preference-fit-registration.json` while collection was still running,
before preference scores were available. Preparation rejects a changed
registration or implementation.

- Rank 16, 5% smooth speaker-input bound, initialization/scheduling seed 4811.
- FP32 DPO, beta 0.1, four pairs per optimizer update, AdamW with zero weight
  decay, peak learning rate 0.003 decaying to 0.0003 over 64 updates, four warmup
  updates, gradient clipping at 1.0. No autocast or TF32.
- Equal speaker sampling, then cycling cases and pairs within each speaker.
  Minimum usable preference coverage: 32 fitting speakers/64 cases and eight
  validation speakers/12 cases. Insufficient coverage prevents fitting.
- Fresh 26-recording CPU evaluations at updates 0, 8, 24 and 64, with pinned
  ASR and original-reference cosine. The full-model unadapted control and
  accepted halfway scores are both retained for comparison.
- Stop after two consecutive checkpoints with no group voice gain plus any
  CER regression, or a worst-clip cosine decline exceeding 0.005 against the
  full-model control. Also stop after two consecutive validation preference-
  loss rises above 0.001 while fitting loss falls by over 0.001. A joint goal
  screening pass stops fitting for broader confirmation, not promotion.
- Save optimizer moments, RNG states and adapter weights atomically; verify
  frozen acoustic hashes, trajectory/reference provenance, displacement and
  generated-speech hashes. Record fitting/validation accuracy, loss gap and
  per-token likelihood drift separately from actual speech quality.

All 87 training tests passed, including a tiny-Qwen CUDA test showing that
save/reload continuation produces matching adapter weights to uninterrupted
training with FP32 Adam moments. The corpus audit independently re-extracts
voice embeddings, checks transcript tokens and reference codecs, recomputes
edit counts and rebuilds all preference pairs before allowing fitting.

Checkpoint zero initialized successfully on the audited corpus: fitting and
validation preference loss both equal log(2), accuracy is 50%, every initial
policy/reference log-ratio is zero, and speaker-input displacement is zero.
The fresh full-model control completed and passed its independent audit:
development CER/WER 4.28%/12.64%, cosine 0.984565; custom CER/WER 3.27%/10.94%,
cosine 0.975648. These are the 26-recording screening panel, not the broader
44-target development benchmark. All hashes and aggregates passed; audio
cosine recomputation differed by at most 1.20e-7. The goal fails at baseline.

At update 8, speaker-balanced fitting preference loss/accuracy is
0.663973/71.11%; preference-validation loss/accuracy is 0.678655/71.93%.
The loss gap is 0.014683. Mean/max relative speaker-input displacement is
1.049%/1.293%, within the 5% bound. Peak allocated GPU memory was
3,887,730,688 bytes. This indicates learning on the preference objective,
not by itself improvement in generated speech.

The complete update-8 screening panel subsequently measured:

| Group | Full-model control CER / WER | Update 8 CER / WER | Control cosine | Update 8 cosine |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Development | 4.28% / 12.64% | 3.63% / 8.62% | 0.984565 | 0.984889 |
| Custom reference | 3.27% / 10.94% | 1.87% / 6.25% | 0.975648 | 0.979494 |

Both group averages improved against the full-model control, but development
CER and custom-reference cosine still fail the requested thresholds. Results
also vary substantially by seed: development CER is 5.96% and 1.30%; custom
CER is 3.27% and 0.47%. These are screening observations, not evidence of a
general improvement across arbitrary texts or seeds.

The worst paired clip cosine decreased by 0.005345 (dev-005, seed 2026,
0.989871 to 0.984525, with zero CER at both checkpoints). This triggers the
first of two consecutive speech-regression warnings in the registered stop
rule despite better group averages. The pipeline continued to update 24 as
registered. The independent update-8 audio/provenance audit passed all 26
recordings: artifact hashes, FP32 adapter and optimizer, frozen acoustic
weights, ASR edit counts and aggregates all match. Re-extracted cosine differs
by at most 1.20e-7; ASR inference was not rerun. Maximum evaluated speaker-input
displacement is 1.11345%, below the registered 5% bound.

Update 24 fitting finished with preference loss/accuracy 0.562056/85.97%
and preference-validation loss/accuracy 0.612143/84.69%. The loss gap is
0.050087; mean/max input displacement is 3.835%/4.175%. Validation loss still
improves, so this is not the registered preference-overfitting condition.
The complete update-24 speech evaluation subsequently measured development
CER/WER 4.75%/12.64%, cosine 0.985096; custom CER/WER 1.87%/7.81%, cosine
0.980299. Development CER regressed from update 8 and from the unadapted
full-model control. Custom mean voice improved again but still misses 0.984.
The worst clip cosine decline versus control was 0.005584, producing the
second consecutive registered speech-regression warning. The pipeline stopped
at 24; update 64 was not trained. This is a generated-speech regression despite
improving preference-validation loss, not the registered preference-loss
overfitting condition.

The independent update-24 audit passed all 26 recordings, frozen weights,
FP32 adapter/optimizer state, artifact hashes and ASR edit aggregates.
Re-extracted cosines differed by at most 1.20e-7; maximum evaluated input
displacement was 3.98282%. The original pipeline process exited successfully.
No candidate passes the joint goal. The improvement at update 8 warrants
investigating fresh samples from that changed policy rather than resuming the
stopped fit on its unchanged initial-policy preference trajectories. A separate
[refreshed stage](0.6b-refreshed-speaker-preferences.md) is now registered and
collecting samples. It has not yet established a speech-quality improvement.

## What changed in the speech errors

A later read-only audit recomputed every ASR edit count across five matched
26-recording candidates and verified their saved audio hashes. In development,
update 8 has 39 character errors over 1,074 reference characters; update 24
has 51. With a fixed minimum-edit alignment, substitutions increase from
14 to 29, deletions from 11 to 12, while insertions fall from 14 to 10.
Space-related edits increase from four to eight. Removing spaces only for a
diagnostic still shows deterioration, from 3.91% to 4.89%; these are **not**
replacement selection scores. Official CER remains 3.63% and 4.75%.

The largest paired increase is dev-009 at seed 2026, with 12 additional
character errors, while its cosine rises from 0.982140 to 0.989253. Thus a
better speaker score can coincide with substantially worse transcript fidelity
in an individual generation. ASR hypotheses are not phonetic transcriptions,
and equally optimal alignments may attribute edit types differently. This
diagnostic motivates joint speech evaluation, not fitting on these development
sentences. The external `joint-error-analysis/report.html` includes matched
audio and transcripts; its JSON contains all 130 records and provenance.

The accepted halfway voice remains selected. No checkpoint has passed the
joint goal. The unchanged final goal is CER below 3% and internal
cosine above 0.984 **in both development and custom groups**, followed by the
previously registered broader confirmation panel.
