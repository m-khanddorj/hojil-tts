# Centered speaker-dependent residual

The existing rank-16 speaker adapter mostly learns a common input shift:
98.26% of original update-8 correction energy is explained by the mean shift
across fitting speakers. Refreshed update 4 still has 98.83% common energy and
worsens development CER. This motivates a separate parameterization that can
learn different corrections from differences between reference speakers. It
does not establish that the existing adapter collapses generated voices.

The new candidate starts from audited original update 8. Its acoustic weights,
speaker encoder, codec and original speaker adapter all remain frozen. Only a
new 1024-by-16 residual matrix is trainable: 16,384 FP32 parameters. A zero
matrix preserves the original policy. No model has been promoted.

## Coordinates and bound

Use the same 95 fitting references from 48 fitting speakers as the refreshed
pool. Normalize each reference vector, average within each speaker, then give
each speaker equal weight when fitting a centered PCA. Preference-validation,
development and custom references do not fit these coordinates. Sixteen fixed
directions explain 79.64% of fitting-speaker variance. Divide coordinates by
their fitting standard deviations and sqrt(16); this gives the learned matrix
access to speaker differences without the large common embedding mean.

Add the learned coordinate-dependent residual to the original adapter's raw
correction, then apply the original smooth 5% relative L2 bound. Thus the total
adjustment, not each component separately, remains bounded. The original
adapter parameters and PCA buffers are checked separately from acoustic
weights: the historical frozen-parameter hash would otherwise also include
the newly frozen original adapter parameters.

This is a diagnostic-driven experiment, not a reproduction of a weight-space
programming paper or a discovered speaker circuit. A fitted input basis and a
low-rank residual do not themselves imply better generated speech.

## Planned comparison

- Reuse the independently audited 508 refreshed trajectories and 477 joint
  preference pairs; original update 8 remains the frozen DPO reference.
- Fresh FP32 AdamW, at most 24 updates, checkpoints 0/8/16/24, seed 11017.
- Sixteen speaker-balanced pairs per update, covering all 48 fitting speakers
  every three updates. Learning rate 0.001 to 0.0001, two warmup updates,
  beta 0.1, gradient clip 1.0, zero weight decay.
- Same all-codebook likelihood and original-reference pronunciation/voice
  preferences. No training on evaluation texts or target audio.
- Same 26-recording CPU FP32 screen at every checkpoint. Stage zero must
  reproduce all original update-8 audio hashes. Stop after two consecutive
  checkpoints where either group has worse CER and no voice gain, or the worst
  clip loses more than 0.005 cosine. This tightens the previous cross-group
  rule, which could overlook deterioration in the custom group when development
  mean cosine improved slightly. Keep the preference-overfit rule and the
  114-recording confirmation requirement unchanged.

Centering, freezing the original adapter, increasing batch coverage and
extending the schedule change together. Any gain would establish the combined
recipe's result, not isolate one component's causal effect. Preference loss
and validation accuracy remain optimization diagnostics; actual CER/WER and
voice similarity determine success.

## Baseline verification and first fitted checkpoint

All 26 stage-zero recordings reproduce original update 8 exactly. The
independent audit verifies FP32 state, frozen acoustic/original-adapter/PCA
components, audio hashes, ASR edits and original-reference cosines. Maximum
cosine recomputation difference is 1.19e-7. Development CER/WER is
3.6313%/8.6207%, cosine 0.984889; custom CER/WER is 1.8692%/6.25%, cosine
0.979494. The baseline does not pass the joint goal.

Update 8 fitting completed with preference loss/accuracy 0.650608/83.35% and
validation loss/accuracy 0.691758/54.01%. The 0.041150 loss gap and near-chance
validation accuracy indicate weak transfer of the learned preferences so far,
not proof of generated-speech regression.

The incremental correction is now speaker-dependent: only 0.0113% of fitting
correction energy and 5.07% of preference-validation correction energy are
explained by a common shift. Mean incremental input displacement is
0.462%/0.350% for fitting/validation speakers. The original adapter and PCA
remain exactly frozen. This verifies the intended parameterization change;
it does not establish better pronunciation or voice fidelity.

Update 8's complete speech panel subsequently improved both group CER and
mean voice similarity relative to stage zero:

| Group | Baseline CER / WER | Update 8 CER / WER | Baseline cosine | Update 8 cosine |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Development | 3.63% / 8.62% | 3.54% / 9.77% | 0.984889 | 0.985470 |
| Custom reference | 1.87% / 6.25% | 1.17% / 1.56% | 0.979494 | 0.980313 |

The development CER change is only one fewer character error (39 to 38 over
1,074 characters), and its WER rises. Custom character errors fall from eight
to five over 428 characters. These small screening counts should not be read
as broad pronunciation guarantees. Development CER and custom voice cosine
still fail the requested thresholds. The worst clip cosine decline is
0.003972, below the registered 0.005 harm limit; no group triggers the joint
regression rule. Fitting proceeds to update 16 as registered.

Independent update-8 verification passes all 26 recordings, artifact hashes,
FP32 optimizer state and frozen acoustic/original-adapter/PCA components.
Recomputed original-reference cosines differ by at most 1.19e-7; maximum
evaluation input displacement is 1.146%. No checkpoint has been promoted.

A separate separation check compares all 127 preference conditions against
all 57 registered confirmation conditions, including both target and reference
transcripts. There are zero normalized-text, speaker-ID or reference-audio-hash
overlaps in either fitting or preference-validation. The external receipt is
`preference-confirmation-separation.json`; it does not audit original model
pretraining/SFT exposure or make the confirmation set an untouched test set.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 | 1.68% / 5.75% | 0.984636 | 3.74% / 9.38% | 0.977098 |

Development passes both screen thresholds at updates 16 and 24. The custom
group deteriorates on both CER and cosine relative to stage zero at both
checkpoints, producing two consecutive registered regression warnings. The
pipeline stops at 24 and exits successfully. This is not a joint-goal pass,
and no development-only confirmation or promotion is substituted for it.

At update 16, fitting/validation preference loss is 0.624130/0.691017 and
accuracy 89.58%/53.59%. At 24 it is 0.616321/0.690869 and 91.53%/51.77%.
The widening preference-loss gap does not trigger the specific validation-
loss-rise rule because validation loss still falls slightly. The decisive
stopping evidence is the generated custom speech regression.

Update 16's independent audit passes all 26 recordings and frozen/FP32 state
checks; maximum re-extracted cosine difference is 1.19e-7 and maximum
evaluation input displacement is 1.182%. Update 24's independent audit also
passes all 26 recordings and parameter-state checks, with maximum cosine
difference 1.19e-7 and evaluation input displacement 1.194%.
Input geometry is verified through 24 with frozen original adapter and PCA
coordinates. The separate reference-prefix routing comparison is prepared
from update 16: it already passes development, and its custom degradation is
smaller than update 24's. No routing quality result exists yet.
