# Joint pronunciation and reference-voice preference pilot

The user goal is **CER <3% and internal speaker cosine >0.984 in both the
development and custom-reference groups**. The halfway model retains the
preferred voice but misses pronunciation. Plain codec-CE continuation and
adapter-strength changes have not met both objectives. This pilot changes
the training objective to preferences between generated speech samples,
following the evidence reviewed in [the literature review](0.6b-joint-goal-literature.md).

## Data and separation

The registered pool contains 16 hash-selected original-training speakers,
two texts per speaker and four fixed generation seeds: 128 candidate samples.
Twelve speakers supply fitting cases; four are held out from this preference
fit. These speakers participated in historical SFT, so this split is not a new
unseen-speaker test. Actual development and custom-reference checks remain
separate. Custom screening transcripts are excluded from rollout selection.
The selected preference splits have no normalized transcript overlap.

Each candidate retains its actual sampled 16-codebook trajectory, sampled
first-codebook sequence, reference codes and speaker embedding, target and
reference text IDs, waveform and checksums. Generation capture verifies that
the first codebook matches the sampled sequence. Audio is not re-encoded and
misrepresented as the original trajectory. Truncated generations remain in
collection summaries but cannot receive fabricated EOS supervision.

Collection completed with all **128 recordings** and **zero truncated samples**.
The pinned filter produced **74 fitting pairs across 22 cases** and **25
validation pairs across all eight validation cases**. Two fitting cases did not
produce a valid joint preference. Verification recomputed the ASR edit counts
and all preference pairs, checked every audio and trajectory hash, and found
no transcript-overlap exclusions. The bounded pilot was then launched; this
data-collection result is not a checkpoint quality improvement.

Only generated-versus-generated pairs under identical conditions are eligible.
The preferred candidate must have no more character errors and no lower
speaker cosine. A pair must correct at least one character or improve cosine
by at least 0.001. Cases are sampled uniformly during fitting; cases with many
valid pair combinations do not receive proportionately larger weight.
All data and checkpoints remain outside Git.

## Objective and numerical checks

The initial policy and frozen reference policy are the exact accepted halfway
model, using the existing protected target-text LoRA routing. There are
114,688 trainable FP32 parameters. Reference transcript and control projections,
the original speaker encoder, codec and speech backbone remain frozen.

Standard DPO uses the difference between chosen and rejected policy-to-reference
log probability ratios, with beta 0.1. Log probabilities sum all 16 codebooks
and the terminal EOS; they are not the historical residual-weighted CE and are
not divided by sequence length. These probabilities describe the raw model,
not the temperature/top-k sampling distribution used to collect candidates.
No real-audio CE auxiliary objective is included in this initial pilot.

Training uses FP32 forwards, parameters and optimizer state, without autocast
or TF32. Reference likelihoods are cached once at initialization. Adapter
dropout is disabled for likelihood comparisons; decoder gradient checkpointing
remains active. The backward pass first computes the DPO scalar derivative,
then recomputes one trajectory at a time to avoid keeping two full graphs in
GPU memory. It checks deterministic likelihood replay before backpropagating.
Tests compare its gradients with the ordinary full-graph DPO loss, including
the actual checkpointed Qwen path, and verify that frozen parameters get no
gradients. A small absolute FP32 reduction tolerance is explicit in the code.

The full 0.6B model also passed a CPU FP32 backward smoke check on two actual
generated trajectories: initial DPO loss was exactly log(2), likelihood replay
passed, both zero-initialized LoRA B factors received finite nonzero gradients,
and frozen parameters stayed unchanged with no gradients. No optimizer update
or weights were saved by that diagnostic. The training-related suite passes
73 tests. Neither check is evidence of improved generated speech.

The bounded run schedules 24 optimizer updates, four pairs per update,
learning rate 5e-5 decaying to 1e-5 after two warmup updates, no weight decay,
gradient clipping at 1.0, and checkpoints at updates 4, 8, 16 and 24. These are
updates, not full dataset epochs. Each saved update checkpoint receives all
26 existing development/custom recordings, both fixed seeds, pinned ASR and
the unchanged internal speaker encoder.

## Observability and stopping

The report includes:

- Development and custom CER, WER and speaker cosine, including seedwise scores.
- Case-balanced training and preference-validation DPO loss, margin and accuracy.
- Validation-minus-training loss gap, chosen/rejected sequence lengths and
  likelihood changes, gradient norm, adapter change norm, learning rate and
  peak GPU allocation.
- Reference/control projection protection checks, immutable input and output
  hashes, and listening examples at every evaluated checkpoint.

Mean absolute log-ratio per token is labeled as a drift diagnostic, **not** an
estimated KL divergence. Preference accuracy counts an exact tie as 50%.
Falling training loss alone cannot justify selecting a model.

Stop after two successive voice-guard failures (either group cosine at or
below 0.984, or any paired recording dropping by more than 0.005). Also stop
after three successive checkpoint intervals where preference-validation loss
rises by more than 0.001 while training loss falls by more than 0.001. A joint
goal screen pass stops the bounded run for broader confirmation. No checkpoint
is automatically promoted to the global selected model.

## Confirmation fixed before seeing pilot results

`training/joint_confirmation.py` has registered an expanded panel outside Git:
all 44 existing development targets with two seeds (**88 recordings**) and
13 custom-reference texts with two seeds (**26 recordings**). The custom texts
include the original three and ten hash-selected development transcripts.
This is expanded development/personalization evidence, not a new untouched
test set. The panel, seeds and generation settings are fixed before preference
checkpoint results are available. A successful candidate must satisfy both
thresholds separately in both groups on the complete panel.

## Entry points

`python -m training.preference_rollouts --parent PARENT --output POOL`
collects and scores the training-only pool. `python -m training.preference_pilot
--pool POOL --output RUN` verifies that completed pool, initializes the frozen
reference likelihoods, trains and evaluates the bounded pilot. The prepared
protocol pins source hashes, so a running experiment cannot silently change
implementation. The HTML report and JSON receipts are written inside the
external run folder. Preparation and tests are implementation evidence, not
evidence that the speech goal has been achieved.

## First checkpoint status

Initialization reproduced zero adapter change, zero policy/reference log-ratio,
and DPO loss log(2) on both splits. The first four FP32 updates then completed
without a numerical failure. At update 4, case-balanced fitting loss was
0.692457 and accuracy 71.44%; preference-validation loss was 0.693126 and
accuracy 57.29% (both started at 50%). The loss gap was 0.000669, adapter change
L2 was 0.027805, and peak allocated GPU memory was 3,963,399,168 bytes.

The direct reference/control projection audit remained exactly zero; target
projection RMS change was 3.5513e-6. These are numerical and learning diagnostics,
not proof of pronunciation or perceptual voice improvement.

All 26 records were audited independently: waveform/reference/model hashes,
ASR edit counts, aggregate metrics and FP32 adapter/optimizer state passed.
Cosines were recomputed directly from the audio using the unchanged original
speaker encoder, with maximum numerical discrepancy 1.79e-7. Both group means
remain above 0.984; worst paired clip change was -0.000512. The new absolute
voice guard passes, but pronunciation has not improved and the goal fails.
The stricter historical no-mean-decrease gate is retained for comparison and
does not pass. No model was promoted.

The scheduled run continued to update 8. Fitting preference accuracy reached
81.06%, but preference-validation accuracy returned to 50%; losses were
0.691647 and 0.693165 respectively. This is no evidence of better speech and
suggests caution about fitting the small preference pool. Update 8 also failed
to improve pronunciation: both development seeds had higher CER than baseline,
while custom CER/WER were unchanged. All 26 update-8 records passed the same
independent audit; cosine recomputation differed by at most 2.38e-7. The worst
paired clip cosine change was -0.000686. The run continued to the registered
update-16 checkpoint, whose fitting/validation preference accuracies are
86.36%/52.08%. Its generated pronunciation also regressed: development CER
remained 10.89%, and custom CER increased to 8.18%. The absolute voice guard
still passes. The final scheduled update-24 checkpoint completed with exactly
the same 26 waveform hashes as update 16. Its fitting preference accuracy rose
to 90.68%, while preference-validation accuracy was 48.96%. There is no evidence
supporting a longer continuation of this small-pool fit.

All four evaluated checkpoints passed the independent audit: **104 new
recordings**, plus the explicitly reused 26-recording halfway baseline. The
completed run's `decision.json` keeps the existing halfway voice, promotes no
model and ends this particular fit. The overall two-group goal remains active.
The separate protected target-weight-transfer comparison also completed without
passing the joint goal: development/custom CER was 7.54%/13.55%. These
negative results do not establish that preference optimization generally fails
for Mongolian TTS; this pilot used only 22 fitting cases and text-projection
LoRA capacity.

A composition audit found 47 pronunciation-improving and 27 voice-only fitting
pairs; 22 of the first 32 sampled update pairs improved pronunciation. Thus the
early lack of speech improvement is not explained by training predominantly on
voice-only pairs. The bounded pilot completed under its registered stopping
rules without changing the objective midway through the experiment.
