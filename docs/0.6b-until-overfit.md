# Continue FP32 LoRA until an overfit signal

Registered November 9, 2026 following the user's request to continue beyond
the second epoch until signs of overfitting appear. Epoch 2 remains the
provisional speech-quality winner until a later candidate passes comparison.

Start from `fresh-fp32-epoch2/best-checkpoint-001750.pt`, preserving adapters,
Adam moments, step counters and CPU/CUDA RNG. Continue the shuffle from epoch
index 2. Keep the learning rate constant at the parent's ending **3e-6**, with
no warmup restart. Rank, dropout, conditioning, loss and data remain unchanged.
The frozen base/forward use BF16; adapter parameters and Adam state use FP32.

## Registered stopping rule

Evaluate the same dropout-disabled matched training, familiar validation and
unseen-speaker validation probes every 100 updates, plus epoch boundaries.
Track each validation cohort's lowest loss and the training loss measured at
that same checkpoint. Stop when either cohort meets both conditions for
**five consecutive checks**:

- Validation loss is at least **0.005 higher** than its prior minimum.
- Matched training loss is at least **0.002 lower** than at that minimum.

Any recovery resets that cohort's counter. A new minimum replaces its anchor.
Repeated evaluation of the same cumulative update does not count twice.
Counters and anchors persist in checkpoints and exact resumes. Non-finite
probes fail closed. This is an operational *possible overfit* signal, not a
statistical proof. A plateau or jointly worsening training/validation loss
does not satisfy it. Either cohort can trigger the stop independently.

The absolute deltas and five-check requirement are experiment choices,
registered before seeing new results. Standard early-stopping implementations
also count validation checks rather than epochs, but ordinarily stop on lack
of improvement; this experiment explicitly requires divergence instead.
See [Lightning's EarlyStopping documentation](https://lightning.ai/docs/pytorch/latest/optional-api/api/lightning.pytorch.callbacks.EarlyStopping).

`configs/qwen06-lora-until-overfit.json` provides a ten-additional-epoch work
block (17,500 updates), not evidence of overfitting if exhausted. If it ends
without a signal, inspect and continue from its final optimizer checkpoint
with the next shuffle epoch and the same guard state. Do not relabel the block
limit as an overfit stop. Save each full epoch and every new best validation
checkpoint, keeping artifacts outside Git. Stop on resource/numerical failure
and report it separately if continuation is not possible.

## Selection and observability

The dashboard includes both validation gaps and guard counters/anchor deltas.
The earliest step of each block retains its unchanged parent as a candidate.
On stopping, verify the best checkpoint, then score its generated speech on
the existing 44 prompts at fixed seeds 2026 and 2027. Compare against epoch 2
using the already cached baseline draws; retain every generated output.
Promotion requires lower pooled WER without pooled CER regression. Report
per-seed scores, speaker-clustered uncertainty, severe errors and blind
listening artifacts. These remain development scores; no independent test or
human preference claim follows automatically.

### Speech-based stopping amendment, registered before epoch 6 results

Per-epoch speech scoring revealed a mismatch between falling teacher-forced
loss and generated-speech performance. In addition to the original automatic
codec-loss guard, stop the trajectory if **three consecutive epoch endpoints**
have strictly worse pooled CER and WER than the selected best endpoint, while
their matched training loss is at least 0.002 lower than at that endpoint.
An improvement resets this speech-regression sequence. All comparisons use
the same full prompts and both fixed seeds. Epochs 4 and 5 are the first two
qualifying observations; epoch 6 has not been scored when this rule is added.

This is a practical early-stopping rule based on repeated deterioration of
the downstream validation proxy. Report it as `speech_regression_patience`,
separately from `overfit_guard`: it is possible overfitting or generation
instability, not proof that teacher-forced validation loss has diverged.
The current supervisor automatically enforces the codec guard; the supervising
agent checks this additional speech rule at each completed evaluation before
allowing further training. Retain the best speech model and evaluate the best
validation-loss checkpoint before closing the experiment.

Epochs 3 and 4 were evaluated on all 44 prompts at both fixed seeds (88
recordings, ten speakers), using the same verified epoch-2 baseline recordings.

Epoch 3 became the provisional development winner. Versus epoch 2, it reduced
word errors by 20 and character errors by 86. Recordings with WER >=50% fell
from three to zero. Its WER change was -2.28 percentage points, with a
speaker-clustered 95% bootstrap interval of [-4.37, -0.57] points.

Epoch 4 was not promoted: WER regressed against epoch 3 on both seeds, and
pooled CER also worsened. WER rose 2.28 points, with a paired speaker-clustered
interval of [+0.11, +4.38] points. There was one recording with WER >=50%.
This is a generated-speech warning despite improving teacher-forced loss;
one regressing epoch does not establish a sustained trajectory.

Epoch 5 partially recovered, with lower CER/WER than epoch 4 on both seeds,
but did not beat epoch 3 in pooled scores. Its WER change against epoch 3 was
+0.80 points, with a speaker-clustered interval of [-1.77, +3.11] points.
Seed 2027 improved while seed 2026 remained worse than epoch 3. End-of-epoch
training/familiar/unseen losses were 3.26484 / 3.33861 / 3.36898, and both overfit
counters were zero. Epoch 3 remained selected while epoch 6 ran under the
same stopping and per-epoch evaluation rules.

These intervals are descriptive of a small, repeatedly used development set;
they are not corrected for all sequential model-selection comparisons. They
do not replace an independent test or blind listening. Epoch 2 and all earlier
checkpoints remain intact. See `epochwise.html` for the current phase, selected
model and accumulated per-epoch results.

## Final decision: stop after epoch 6, retain epoch 3

Epoch 6 again had worse pooled CER and WER than epoch 3, and regressed against
epoch 3 on both seeds. Its WER change was +1.83 percentage points, with a
speaker-clustered 95% interval of [-1.06, +4.99] points. The registered
speech-based patience rule therefore reached three consecutive epochs: 4, 5
and 6. Their matched training losses were 3.27108, 3.26484 and 3.25807, versus
3.27862 at epoch 3. Stop reason: **`speech_regression_patience`**.

The automatic codec-loss overfit guard **did not trigger**; both counters
remained zero. At epoch 6, familiar/unseen validation losses were
3.33190 / 3.36676, still below earlier epochs. The familiar and unseen
training-validation gaps were 0.07383 and 0.10869. This is an operational stop
for persistent deterioration of the speech proxy while fitting training data
more closely. It is compatible with overfitting or generation instability,
and does not prove classical teacher-forced validation divergence.

The lowest combined validation loss in the continued trajectory was
**3.34933 at total update 10,500**, the end of epoch 6. Tensor equality confirmed
that this checkpoint's adapter was exactly the already evaluated epoch-6
endpoint; its verified recordings were reused, avoiding an additional draw.
It did not beat epoch 3. The final selected model is
**`fresh-fp32-epoch3`**, with **3.27% CER / 9.02% WER** on the pooled development
comparison. This is about 20.2% lower WER than epoch 2. It remains provisional
pending independent evaluation and human listening.

Six complete epochs correspond to 10,500 updates and 168,006 clip exposures on
the accepted trajectory. The already queued epoch-7 process was stopped during
initialization, before its initial checkpoint or training updates. No active
training job remains. All completed epoch checkpoints and the earlier partial
epoch-5 checkpoint remain intact outside Git.

## Later exploratory test evidence

The subsequent six-epoch reserved-test expansion found epoch 6 had the lowest
observed pooled CER (3.65%) and WER (8.98%), despite its development regression.
The earlier stopping decision was therefore a development-based warning, not
proof that later epochs generalize worse everywhere. The epoch-6 versus
 epoch-2 WER change was -1.95 percentage points, with a speaker-bootstrap 95%
interval of [-8.11, +2.08] points, including no improvement. This expanded
ranking is exploratory because the test had already been inspected; it does
not justify retuning against this test or silently changing model selection.
