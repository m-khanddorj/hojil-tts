# Refreshed speaker-preference stage

The first bounded speaker-adapter experiment improved both group means at
update 8, then increased development CER at update 24 despite decreasing
preference-validation loss. It stopped under its registered regression rule.
Its audited update 8 is the starting policy for a separate short stage, not
a promoted model or a reset to the original full-LoRA policy.

[Beyond Fixed Targets](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17838v1), rechecked November
11, studies current-policy trajectories in English one-shot LLaSA adaptation.
It reports gains from GRPO and on-policy self-distillation, and tests reuse
of sampled trajectories to reduce generation cost. This motivates refreshing
our samples but does not establish that stale samples caused our regression.
Our method remains staged DPO with joint pronunciation/voice preferences,
not a reproduction of its GRPO or self-distillation methods.

## Registered collection

`training/refreshed_speaker_rollouts.py` preserves the original 127 fitting/
validation cases across 64 speakers: 95 cases from 48 fitting speakers and
32 from 16 preference-validation speakers. Cases, reference audio, transcripts,
split assignments and generation settings are unchanged. Four draws per case
use new fixed batch seeds `17301 + case_index`, producing 508 trajectories.
No development/custom examples are introduced into fitting.

The policy includes the original full epoch-2 acoustic LoRA weights plus the
nonzero speaker adapter from audited update 8. Acoustic weights and the
speaker adapter must remain unchanged during collection. Rewards and stored
conditioning use the original reference embedding, not the adjusted speaker
input. Every sampled codec trajectory and first-codebook EOS is retained and
checked. GPU FP32 batched generation remains distinct from CPU screening.

The reconstructed CPU control reproduces update-8 custom-001 at seed 2026
byte-for-byte. Unit checks verify unchanged case/speaker allocation, new RNG
streams, nonzero checkpoint loading and adapter-state hashing.

The initial four-sample GPU batch completed with four distinct waveforms,
natural EOS in every sample, matching sampled codec sequences and verified
audio/trajectory hashes. Peak allocated GPU memory was about 3.98 GB. The
full sequential collection/scoring/audit/fitting pipeline is now running;
three new focused tests passed, including the CUDA optimizer-resume test.

## Registered fitting

`training/refreshed_speaker_training.py` starts from that same nonzero adapter,
with freshly initialized FP32 AdamW moments and frozen DPO reference
likelihoods from update 8. Settings and source hashes were registered before
collection scores existed in the external `refreshed-speaker-fit-registration.json`.

- At most 16 additional updates; checkpoints at 0, 4, 8 and 16.
- Four speaker-balanced pairs per update; scheduling seed 9017.
- Learning rate 0.001 to 0.0001, two warmup updates, beta 0.1, clipping 1.0,
  zero weight decay. Original rank 16 and 5% absolute input bound remain.
- Audit all 508 recordings, codec trajectories, reference conditioning, ASR
  edit counts, cosines and joint preference pairs before fitting. The original
  minimum pair-coverage requirements remain in force.
- Generate and score all 26 fixed development/custom recordings at every
  checkpoint. Stage zero must reproduce all original update-8 waveforms.
- Use the same two-consecutive-regression and preference-loss stopping rules,
  relative to this stage's starting policy. No automatic model promotion.

Refreshing samples, resetting the preference reference/optimizer and reducing
the learning rate happen together; a gain would not isolate the effect of any
one change. The frozen acoustic model and original speaker encoder remain
unchanged. The CUDA tiny-Qwen test passes from a nonzero initial adapter,
including zero initial DPO log-ratios and matching saved/resumed versus
uninterrupted updates with FP32 optimizer moments.

An additional CPU-only real-model diagnostic compared cached native generation
with the preference teacher-forcing path under the loaded update-8 adapter.
On one original-training prompt, it checked eight main-codebook prediction
steps and 105 residual predictions across seven complete frames. Prefix
embeddings matched exactly; maximum raw-logit differences were 1.38e-5 for
the main head and 1.34e-4 for residual heads. All argmax choices matched.
Summed selected-token raw log probability differed by 0.000153 over 112 tokens,
and acoustic/adapter weights stayed unchanged. No EOS was assigned to the
unfinished final frame. The receipt is external in
`native-preference-likelihood-verification.json`.

This supplements the tiny-model prefix/alignment tests. It is one short CPU
continuation, not proof over all prompts or GPU kernels, and compares raw
model distributions rather than temperature/top-k/repetition-processed
sampling probabilities. It provides no new speech-quality score.

Artifacts stay outside Git in `refreshed-speaker-rollouts/` and, after the
collection audit, `refreshed-speaker-pilot/`. The full goal remains CER below
3% and cosine above 0.984 in both groups, followed by the registered 114-
recording confirmation. No quality improvement is claimed for this stage yet.

## Completed collection and audit

All 508 recordings completed without truncation. Generation verified that both
the frozen acoustic weights and the update-8 speaker adapter were unchanged.
ASR scoring produced 477 valid joint preference pairs. The independent pool
audit checked all audio/trajectory hashes, actual sampled codec sequences and
EOS, reference codecs and embeddings, transcript tokens, stored ASR edit counts
and rebuilt preference pairs. Re-extracted cosines differed by at most 4.77e-7.

Fitting has 357 pairs across 94 cases and all 48 fitting speakers; 199 pairs
improve pronunciation. One of the 95 collected fitting cases had no eligible
pair. Validation has 120 pairs across all 32 cases/16 speakers, with 61
pronunciation-improving pairs. Both coverage requirements pass.

For observability only, all-candidate fitting-pool CER/WER is 6.42%/14.80%,
speaker-macro cosine 0.979898; validation-pool CER/WER is 4.83%/12.23%, cosine
0.981006. These use four new GPU batch draws per training case. They are not
development/custom evaluation results, and changed seeds prevent an exact
paired policy comparison with the previous pool.

Stage-zero initialization completed: fitting/validation DPO loss is log(2),
accuracy is 50%, all initial reference log-ratios are zero, and adapter change
from update 8 is zero. Absolute input displacement remains about 1.05% on
average, confirming the nonzero starting adapter was retained. The pipeline
completed the stage-zero speech control: all 26 waveforms reproduce original
update 8 exactly. Independent verification passed for checkpoint/optimizer
precision, frozen acoustic weights, audio hashes, stored ASR edits and
re-extracted original-reference cosines (maximum difference 1.19e-7).
Development CER/WER is 3.6313%/8.6207%, cosine 0.984889; custom CER/WER is
1.8692%/6.25%, cosine 0.979494. This control fails the joint goal.

Additional update 4 has fitting/validation preference loss 0.685710/0.689201
and accuracy 70.56%/64.64%. Its full speech evaluation and independent audit
are complete: development CER/WER 5.4004%/13.7931%, cosine 0.985295; custom
CER/WER 2.1028%/4.6875%, cosine 0.979139. Development CER and custom voice
regress from stage zero. The worst clip cosine decline is 0.005541, triggering
the first registered speech-regression warning. All 26 artifacts, FP32 states,
frozen acoustic weights and recomputed metrics pass verification; cosine
re-extraction differs by at most 1.19e-7. The pipeline continues to additional
update 8 under its registered stop rule. The accepted halfway model remains
selected.

Additional update 8 is also complete and independently verified. Development
CER/WER is 7.6350%/17.2414%, cosine 0.985091; custom CER/WER is
7.7103%/18.75%, cosine 0.978850. Preference-validation loss nevertheless falls
to 0.684275, accuracy 66.46%, illustrating objective mismatch again. The
registered cross-group stop rule does not flag this checkpoint: development
mean voice is slightly above stage zero and the worst clip decline (0.004786)
is below 0.005. The pipeline therefore completes its final update-16 evaluation.
This is adherence to the existing protocol, not endorsement of update 8.

A separate [centered residual](0.6b-centered-speaker-preferences.md) is being
prepared. Its prospective stopping rule checks each group for worse CER with
no voice gain, so slight improvement in one group cannot hide deterioration
in the other. The current registered run is not retroactively changed.

## Input-space diagnostic

A separate read-only check uses all 127 stored training/pref-validation
reference vectors, averaging within each speaker and then weighting speakers
equally. Original update 8's relative input corrections are nearly parallel:
mean pairwise correction cosine is 0.99937 across fitting speakers and 0.99970
across preference-validation speakers. The common mean accounts for 98.26%
and 98.47% of correction energy, respectively. At original update 24 those
fractions are 99.76%/99.78%; refreshed update 4 is 98.83%/98.86%.

This shows the current adapter mostly learns a shared input shift despite
having rank 16. It does not establish that generated voices collapse: mean
pairwise reference-input cosine changes by less than 0.0005, and output speech
must still be measured. The diagnostic uses no development/custom references,
loads no acoustic model, and changes no weights. Its external receipt is
`speaker-adapter-geometry.json`. A centered, speaker-dependent residual is a
possible later experiment; no such candidate has been trained or scored.
