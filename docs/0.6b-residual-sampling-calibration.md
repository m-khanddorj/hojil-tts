# Residual-code sampling calibration

This separately registered decoding experiment follows the calibrated
supervised-loss comparison. It freezes the audited unfiltered middle-attention
update-4 model, including the original update-8 speaker adapter. That candidate
has development CER 2.05% and cosine 0.98506, and custom-reference CER 1.64% and
cosine 0.97910. Its custom voice fails the required threshold; it is not promoted.

## Mechanism and limits

Qwen has independent main-talker and residual-predictor sampling settings.
The local implementation passes the residual settings to the predictor for
the remaining 15 codebooks; their embeddings are summed and fed into the next
main-talker step. Thus reducing residual randomness could affect both voice
and pronunciation, and is not an acoustic-only intervention. The earlier
temperature experiment changed the main talker, not this residual temperature.
[Official implementation](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/main/qwen_tts/core/models/modeling_qwen3_tts.py).

The hypothesis is that residual temperature 0.6 may preserve the reference voice
better than 0.9 without losing the frozen model's pronunciation. This is an
untested inference from the implementation, not a published performance claim.
No weight-space or training improvement is claimed.

## Fixed comparison

- Control residual temperature 0.9 versus candidate 0.6. Both use sampling,
  residual top-k 50 and top-p 1. Main sampling stays at temperature 0.9,
  top-k 50, top-p 1 and at most 320 new tokens.
- Four speakers selected by a fixed hash from the preference-validation split,
  two distinct cases per speaker, both seeds 2026 and 2027: 16 recordings per
  arm, 32 total. These speakers were excluded from preference fitting but
  appeared in original supervised training; this is calibration, not unseen
  test evidence. Target and reference transcripts differ.
- No speaker ID, normalized transcript or reference-audio hash overlaps the
  registered development/custom confirmation conditions.
- CPU FP32 SDPA synthesis with four threads, full reference audio/text ICL,
  original-reference cosine and the same separate FP32 GPU Mongolian ASR.
- A native control must exactly reproduce an existing audited update-4 WAV.
  Every real predictor call is observed to verify the intended residual
  settings. Both adapters and all original model parameters remain frozen.

Expansion requires strictly higher speaker-macro cosine, no pooled CER or WER
increase, no individual cosine decrease exceeding 0.005, and termination of
every recording. An independent phase re-extracts voice embeddings from the
stored audio and recomputes edit counts, coverage and the gate. It verifies
audio/trajectory hashes, sampled codebooks/EOS and model identity. ASR inference
is not rerun during this audit. There is no best-of-N selection or dropped seed.

A calibration pass would authorize the full 26-recording development/custom
screen, followed by all 114 existing confirmation conditions if the screen
passes. The changed residual generation policy must be declared in a derived
registration; the original protocol is not edited. Both groups still require
CER strictly below 3% and original-reference cosine strictly above 0.984.
Calibration cannot complete the goal or change the accepted model.

## Implementation and status

`training/residual_sampling_calibration.py` is a new module; historical and
currently running registered modules remain unchanged. Three focused tests
pass: deterministic split selection; actual-call observation and restoration
with unchanged main settings; and rejection of incomplete, truncated or harmful
calibration comparisons. A preparation guard initially rejected the parent's
explicit `subtalker_dosample=True`; inspection verified it matches the intended
control. The guard now accepts only matching explicit defaults. No audio or
model update occurred before the final successful registration.

The prepared protocol, external sequential driver and test log are under the
experiment artifact root, outside Git:
`residual-temperature-calibration/protocol.json`,
`run-residual-temperature-calibration.py`, and `residual-sampling-tests.log`.
The driver refuses to start while the calibrated fit owns compute, and refuses
to overwrite or implicitly resume existing execution. The calibrated fit has
now stopped at update 16 after its second independently verified speech-harm
warning, and its original process exited successfully. The residual-temperature
driver has been launched. Its native control exactly reproduced the existing
`2026-custom-001.wav` (SHA-256
`5e4c40d7c8c00b6a0d4a7ac3ff0388b31b295f6cddec288c579b412f237d52f4`).
All 63 observed residual predictor calls used the expected control settings.
Exact parity on this control validates that loading and observation did
not change that waveform, not a broader speech-quality claim.

All 32 calibration recordings are now scored and independently audited. The
original driver exited successfully.

| Residual temperature | CER | WER | Original-reference cosine |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.9 control | 5.2581% | 15.2439% | 0.98357591 |
| 0.6 candidate | 4.9713% | 12.8049% | 0.98424578 |

The averages improve, but the worst recording loses 0.00794077 cosine, exceeding
the registered 0.005 limit. The calibration expansion gate therefore fails.
Two recordings exceed that limit: `balanced-010`, seed 2026, falls from
0.98101509 to 0.97307432 and adds three character errors; `balanced-021`, seed
2026, falls from 0.98332614 to 0.97647643 despite reducing character errors
from thirteen to six. Neither is excluded from the result.
Seed 2026 CER also worsens from 5.54% to 6.88%, while seed 2027 improves from
4.97% to 3.06%; this is a mixed result, not uniformly improved pronunciation.
Every recording terminates, model weights remain unchanged, and independent
cosine re-extraction discrepancy is zero. No development/custom expansion or
model promotion follows this trial. These calibration aggregates do not prove
the user's two-group goal. `report.html` contains all paired audio and scores.
