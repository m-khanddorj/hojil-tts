# Reference-protected pronunciation experiment

The previous text-only LoRA pilot improved pooled development WER at step 44,
but failed the joint screen: custom CER and one seed's WER worsened, and a
development recording lost 0.01652 speaker cosine. The preferred halfway model
therefore remains the baseline.

## Different approach

The pinned Qwen implementation uses the same two-layer text projection for
target words, reference words, role tokens, and TTS BOS/EOS/PAD. In non-streaming
ICL, the projected PAD vector is added to reference and generated audio frames.
The previous adapter consequently changed conditioning beyond the target words.

This experiment routes the fresh LoRA only through target-transcript positions
inside `generate_icl_prompt`. All other projections use the frozen base layers.
Training and generation share this route. Unsupported non-ICL or streaming
generation fails explicitly. The adapter must remain separate: merging it into
the projection matrices would defeat the routing protection.

The full projection is computed with the original tensor shape, then masked,
so zero-initialized LoRA preserves the baseline numerical path. A real-waveform
SHA-256 parity check precedes training. The acoustic backbone, speaker encoder,
embeddings and codec remain frozen. Only four FP32 tensors, 114,688 parameters,
train; training forward/backward retains BF16 autocast.

This is an engineering hypothesis. Keeping direct reference/control embeddings
unchanged does not freeze downstream attention states, prosody or perceived
voice: the target text still conditions speech generation.

## Evidence informing the experiment

Qwen conditioning research finds that ICL can better preserve timbre with
correct reference transcripts, and that objective scores can disagree with
listeners. This supports protecting reference conditioning and retaining
listening checks; it does not establish this routing method as a proven fix.
[Baroudi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23678).

Recent one-shot personalization work reports benefits from on-policy speaker
optimization and reference-conditioned distillation. Its single-reference
setting differs from this multi-speaker pronunciation dataset; it is a possible
future direction, not a method implemented in this run.
[Park et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17838).

## Controlled comparison and checks

Entry point: `python -m training.reference_protected --config configs/qwen06-reference-protected.json`.
The original pilot source is preserved. The new driver explicitly injects the
routed model loader into its unchanged training/evaluation helpers, and child
processes enter the new module. Both source files are hashed and copied into
the run protocol.

Use the previous pilot's exact halfway FP32 backbone, data order, rank 16,
dropout 0.05, AdamW schedule (5e-5 peak), 16 clips/update, maximum 88 updates,
and checkpoints 0/22/44/66/88. Each checkpoint finishes both speech evaluations
before further training. No old adapter or optimizer state is continued.

Each checkpoint generates 26 recordings: ten development speakers and three
custom-reference sentences, each at seeds 2026 and 2027. Regenerate the baseline
and compare its waveform hashes against the prior pilot. Use pinned BuzzASR
CER/WER and speaker-macro Qwen cosine separately for development and custom
voice; retain per-seed and per-recording diagnostics. Do not use reserved-test
scores for selection.

The unchanged joint gate requires lower development pooled WER, no increase in
pooled CER or seedwise WER in either group, no group mean cosine decrease beyond
1e-7, and no recording's cosine decrease greater than 0.005. Stop after two
consecutive voice-screen failures. These are conservative screening rules, not
calibrated perceptual noninferiority criteria. No automatic deployment or global
model promotion occurs.

At each checkpoint, also inspect familiar/unseen validation loss, matched
training loss, gradient norms, learning rate, GPU memory and OOM retries.
`routing-speech-NNNNNN.json` verifies exactly zero direct reference/control
projection change with the actual loaded adapter and records target projection
RMS change on a fixed token probe. Frozen parameter fingerprints are checked
after training and before synthesis.

The 54 training-related tests passed, including real-Qwen tests of unchanged
reference/control projections under nonzero LoRA, zero reference-side adapter
gradients, target-side gradients, exact training/generation prefix agreement,
exception cleanup and unsupported-mode rejection.
An additional checkpoint-audit test passed after instrumentation was added,
verifying that the audit preserves RNG state and training mode: 55 unique tests
passed across the suite and focused rerun.

Stopped for `voice_guard`; selected step 0. Verified all 78 recordings. All 26 initial waveforms, ASR transcripts and cosine scores exactly matched the previous pilot baseline. Reference/control projection changes remained exactly zero, while target projection changes were nonzero after training. Frozen backbone, FP32 optimizer moments, source hashes and paired scoring were verified.

Step 22 screen findings: custom: mean speaker cosine decreased; custom: pooled CER increased; custom: seed 2026 WER increased; development: seed 2027 WER increased.

Step 44 screen findings: custom: mean speaker cosine decreased; A recording exceeded the registered cosine-drop review threshold; custom: seed 2026 WER increased.

The step-44 pooled improvements were development CER/WER 9.31%/23.56% to
7.54%/18.97%, and custom CER/WER 7.24%/23.44% to 6.07%/18.75%. These are
screening-panel results, not a new reserved-test claim. The custom results were
inconsistent across seeds: WER worsened from 18.75% to 28.13% for seed 2026,
while improving from 28.13% to 9.38% for seed 2027.

Development mean speaker cosine rose from 0.98651 to 0.98703, but custom mean
cosine fell from 0.98496 to 0.98374. Custom example 004 at seed 2026 had the
largest decrease, 0.00889, with unchanged CER/WER on that particular recording.
The dashboard includes this pair, alongside the regular example at every
checkpoint. No listening judgments were collected for the new adapter.

Compared with the previous global text-adapter step 44, the new route had lower
pooled development WER (18.97% versus 20.69%) and custom WER (18.75% versus
23.44%), and a smaller worst cosine decrease (0.00889 versus 0.01652). It still
did not meet the registered joint constraints. Exact protection of direct
reference/control projections alone did not eliminate downstream voice changes.

Matched training loss decreased from 3.76352 to 3.75679, familiar validation
loss from 4.11231 to 4.10866, and unseen validation loss from 4.17405 to 4.16915.
There is no demonstrated loss-divergence overfit pattern in this short run;
the stop was triggered by voice screening. The baseline is retained locally,
with no deployment or global model-selection change.

Descriptive bootstrap intervals, resampling the ten development speakers while
keeping both seeds together, cross zero for CER/WER and cosine changes. At
step 44, the development WER difference is -4.60 percentage points, with a
95% percentile interval of approximately -12.21 to +5.23 points. These small-panel
estimates are unadjusted for repeated checkpoint evaluation and do not establish
a population-level improvement. They did not alter the registered selection rule.
