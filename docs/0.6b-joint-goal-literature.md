different halfway/text-adapter replay contract; it cannot be inserted into the

## Research most relevant to the next experiment

### 1. Joint preference alignment is the strongest direct match

**Koel-TTS, EMNLP 2025.** Generated candidates are ranked with CER and speaker
similarity together. Preference pairs are rejected if the chosen example is
worse on either metric. Reward-aware preference optimization also accounts for
the size of the improvement. This closely matches our two-objective problem.
Its encoder-decoder architecture, codec and training scale differ from Qwen;
its reported scores cannot predict ours. Its classifier-free guidance also
depends on conditioning dropout during training, so it is not a justified
drop-in decoding flag for our current checkpoint.
[Paper and methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05236v2).

**Preference Alignment Improves Language Model-Based TTS, ICASSP 2025.**
This work supports selecting both preferred and rejected examples from model
generations. Treating real audio as automatically preferred produced a trivial
discrimination problem. Combining intelligibility, speaker and quality signals
was more balanced than selecting by one metric. It reports all sampled outputs
and checks additional metric models, which is valuable protection against
optimizing only an evaluator's quirks. Its English 1.15B model is not our
Mongolian 0.6B model.
[Methods and ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12403v1).

**Advancing Zero-shot TTS Intelligibility across Diverse Domains, ACL 2025.**
The INTP study applies preference alignment to difficult pronunciation,
repetition, code-switching and cross-lingual cases across several architectures.
It supports creating training examples around actual error categories, rather
than repeating random natural-speech training indefinitely. The released corpus
does not automatically provide the Mongolian/reference-voice supervision we
need; our preference examples must come from our training partition.
[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.598/).

**Decision:** prioritize a bounded joint-preference pilot if the current
strength search misses the target. Generate candidates for training-only
text/reference pairs, preserve the actual generated codec trajectories, and
choose pairs only when one is no worse on CER and cosine and strictly better
on at least one. Never turn the development/custom evaluation outputs into
preference-training examples. Keep normal real-speech supervision as an
explicitly weighted auxiliary objective if used, not as a fake preferred sample.

### 2. Correct the generated-trajectory mismatch

**Beyond Fixed Targets, October 2026 preprint.** On-policy GRPO and
reference-conditioned self-distillation improve one-shot speaker adaptation
in the paper's LLaSA setting. Its central distinction is supervision along
student-generated trajectories, rather than repeatedly predicting fixed
reference targets. Our dataset has many speakers and does not exhibit its
immediate-EOS collapse, so its results are not a direct diagnosis of our run.
However, it strengthens the case for evaluating and training on actual
generated continuations when teacher-forced losses disagree with speech quality.
[Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17838v1).

**Decision:** generated-versus-generated preference training is the practical
first step. Consider refreshed rollouts or on-policy refinement after validating
that pilot. A teacher-forced residual-codebook KL prototype exists locally and
passes alignment/gradient/state-restoration tests, but has **not been trained**.
It is an auxiliary preservation hypothesis, not an implementation of this
paper's on-policy self-distillation and not evidence of better voice quality.

### 3. Use architecture knowledge, without assuming perfect separation

**Qwen3-TTS technical report.** The 12 Hz tokenizer places semantic information
in the first codebook and acoustic refinements in fifteen residual codebooks.
The main decoder predicts the first group; the code predictor supplies residual
groups. This motivates testing content adaptation while constraining acoustic
changes. It does not establish that identity lives only in the residual groups;
our own main-only adaptation already changed the reference voice substantially.
[Architecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15621v1).

**PEFT-TTS, Interspeech 2025.** This F5-TTS study explicitly investigates the
pronunciation/speaker-similarity trade-off during language adaptation. Separate
text-conditioning, prompt and decoder adapters, with prompt DropPath, influence
that balance. Increasing adaptation capacity can trade away unseen-speaker
behavior. F5's flow model and Korean experiments differ from our autoregressive
Mongolian system; its adapter recipe cannot be copied literally.
[Paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/kwon25_interspeech.pdf).

**Decision:** retain our tested protection of reference/control projections and
frozen speaker encoder/codec. Do not assume 114,688 text-adapter parameters are
enough to reach <3% CER. If preference learning saturates there, expand a bounded
part of the main decoder while keeping the voice objective explicit, rather
than unfreezing everything or increasing rank uniformly.

### 4. Token-level pronunciation correction is a later option

**TKTO, ACL 2026.** Token-level preference weights are estimated from two
contrastive policies, trained with normal and reversed desirable/undesirable
labels. This can use unpaired feedback and focus learning on pronunciation
errors. It is not merely ordinary DPO with per-token ASR timestamps. The
demonstrated Japanese/Chinese ambiguity task differs from our errors and the
extra policies increase implementation and compute cost.
[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.59/).

**Decision:** first determine whether our remaining errors concentrate in
repeatable token/word categories. Use TKTO-style credit assignment only if that
diagnosis and a simpler preference baseline justify it.

## Weight-Space Programming: useful, but require a specific mechanism

The original **Sparks of Weight-Space Programming** source is a November 2026
research blog with released logs, not a demonstrated TTS method. Its language
model example repairs a localized repetition mechanism through direct parameter
edits; another model without that pathology did not gain a verified new
capability. The useful lesson is to diagnose a particular failure, patch a small
identified mechanism, and test untouched examples. It does not justify assuming
that arbitrary weight edits will teach missing Mongolian pronunciation.
[Original report and logs](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/).

Recent primary investigations also report inconsistent editing outcomes and
unchanged baselines when gains cannot be validated.
[Cross-task study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382).
A robot-policy study includes matched random-direction controls and many
unsuccessful editing sessions, supporting careful causal comparisons rather
than relying on an editor's interpretation of weight values.
[Robot-policy study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09376).

**Concrete diagnostic completed:** use cached embeddings from training speakers
only, give every speaker equal weight, and estimate their centered variation
subspace. Inspect whether the learned text-output projection update is unusually
aligned with those directions compared with deterministic random subspaces.
The dimensions coincide because this text projection and the speaker prompt
both enter the talker representation. Geometric overlap is still not proof
that a direction causes perceived speaker drift.

The completed diagnostic used **28,001 training clips and 435 equally weighted
speakers**. The top 16 centered speaker directions explain 72.01% of speaker
variation, but only 1.08% of the text-output weight update lies in that subspace,
compared with 1.52% on average for random 16-dimensional subspaces (64 controls).
There is no unusual alignment supporting this particular subtraction edit.
No parameter edit was made from that hypothesis. This rejects one simple
geometric explanation, not Weight-Space Programming as a whole. Results and
the saved basis are in `weight-space-voice-diagnostic` outside Git.

If the diagnostic gives a specific plausible target, test a small, reversible
projection edit to the relevant LoRA factors, plus a matched-norm/random-direction
control and the unedited checkpoint. All variants must pass the same CER/cosine
evaluation. If there is no meaningful alignment, do not invent a speaker circuit
from raw numbers. Component-wise checkpoint blending is another gradient-free
candidate, but should be described as model merging rather than treated as
proof of the original Weight-Space Programming claims.

**Later local evidence:** a mechanism-specific intervention retained the
halfway reference prefix while changing only the two recurrent generation PAD
inputs to their full-model values. All 26 screening prefixes and first codec
frames remained exact against the control. This improved custom screen CER
from 5.61% to 2.80% while retaining cosine above 0.984, but the original full
114-recording confirmation failed: development CER 4.22%, custom CER 3.28%,
and custom cosine 0.98356. The development branch was inactive and therefore
exposed a weakness in the shared fallback, independently of the custom
intervention. A plausible localized mechanism and a passing small screen were
insufficient evidence of the requested broader quality. The next frozen-weight
comparison combines the existing attention-4/speaker-8 adapter with that
conditioning intervention and evaluates all 114 recordings from the outset.
See [the controlled comparison](0.6b-next-reference-policy-probes.md); this is
local inference evidence, not a reproduction of the cited weight-editing work.

## Joint preference data collection

`training/preference_rollouts.py` now collects a registered 128 generated samples:
16 hash-selected training speakers, two texts per speaker, four fixed seeds.
Four of these speakers are reserved for preference-validation, leaving 12 for
fitting. They all come from the original training partition; the actual
development/custom screening examples remain separate. Custom evaluation text
and reference transcripts are explicitly excluded from selection. Selection
occurs before viewing generated scores.

The collector starts from the unchanged halfway voice (protected checkpoint 0).
It captures original sampled codec frames and checks their first codebook
against the actual generation token sequence. It saves the full reference
conditioning alongside each trajectory. Truncated samples are counted but
excluded from preference pairs, rather than fabricated into EOS targets.
Pair filtering uses the pinned ASR and internal cosine, with both candidates
generated under the same conditions. All candidates contribute to collection
summaries, including those that do not form a usable pair.

The collector completed all 128 samples with no truncation. Verification found
74 fitting pairs and 25 preference-validation pairs; the bounded DPO pilot has
been launched. These are CUDA FP32 rollouts
with TF32 disabled, whereas checkpoint selection continues to use the existing
CPU FP32 protocol. This backend difference is recorded, not silently treated as
evaluation equivalence. The current training-related suite passes **75 tests**.
Collection artifacts are outside Git in `joint-preference-rollouts`.

The completed pool and preference-train/validation separation were verified,
and the optimization protocol was registered before fitting. The pilot uses
summed log probabilities across all 16 codebooks plus EOS for
the standard [DPO objective](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18290v3); the historical
0.3-weighted residual CE is not a joint sequence log probability. It keeps reference
likelihoods fixed, disables stochastic adapter dropout for likelihood comparison,
and evaluates generated CER and voice at every saved checkpoint. See the
[pilot protocol and observability](0.6b-joint-preference-training.md). No new
checkpoint speech-quality gain is claimed until its evaluation is complete.

## Further literature: optimize speaker conditioning, not only model weights

Reviewed November 11, 2026 while the controlled weight transfer was evaluating.

**ASRRL-TTS (2024)** learns bounded speaker-embedding adjustments with PPO,
using speaker similarity, predicted quality and intelligibility together.
Its single-reference setting modifies the embedding rather than requiring
global TTS fine-tuning. Experiments use VITS/Grad-TTS and English corpora;
they do not establish effectiveness for Qwen or Mongolian. The relevant lesson
is that the reference representation is an optimization variable, and voice
reward alone can damage intelligibility.
[Primary methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05421v1).

**VoiceTTA (2026 preprint)** optimizes small learned prefixes for F5-TTS at
inference, combining speaker, prosodic and intelligibility rewards. This is
per-prompt adaptation, not a language-model LoRA recipe. Its flow-loss-based
probability proxy should not be copied as an autoregressive likelihood ratio;
Qwen provides actual codec-token log probabilities. Its reported 50 adaptation
steps with four candidates per step also imply substantial sampling cost.
[Primary methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26534v1).

**Completed follow-up on training speakers:** retain the
full epoch-2 model's learned pronunciation and adjust speaker conditioning
through a bounded reference-only reconstruction update. See the
[registered calibration](0.6b-speaker-reference-adaptation.md).
It failed the expansion gate: CER rose from 3.02% to 4.12%, with no mean voice
gain. Lower reference reconstruction loss did not transfer to better different-
text synthesis. No development/custom expansion or model promotion followed.
Reference-only reconstruction or generated-speech rewards are different
experimental objectives and must not be conflated. Any reference-conditioned
test-time adaptation must be declared as such, freeze its hyperparameters on
training speakers, use no evaluation target audio, reset per speaker, and test
new target texts. It would change the adaptation procedure, so its unadapted
control must be measured under the same generation settings. No quality gain
or reproduction of either paper is claimed from this literature review.

## Additional review: disentanglement and its measured limits

Reviewed November 11, 2026 during the reference-audio routing comparison.

- [Activation Steering for Accent-Neutralized Zero-Shot TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05977v1)
  directly studies Qwen3-TTS, including 0.6B. It extracts layerwise differences
  from generated-token activations under native/accented references, and applies
  norm-preserving steering only to generated tokens. Reference augmentation
  helps reduce identity confounding. The 0.6B layer-15 result improves English
  WER from 3.98% to 2.64%, while speaker similarity falls from 0.85 to 0.73.
  It uses Resemblyzer, so those cosine values are not comparable to our internal
  encoder. This supports testing localized interventions, but provides no
  evidence that steering automatically preserves our voice threshold. We do
  not have the paper's matched native/accented Mongolian extraction corpus.
- [UtterTune](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09767v2) adds attention LoRA and two
  phoneme-mode tokens to CosyVoice2, with Japanese pronunciation annotations.
  It improves controlled pitch-accent accuracy and retains speaker similarity.
  Everyday-text CER actually rises from 5.57% to 6.39%; on difficult words,
  simple kana input gives 5.95%, versus the adapted model's 6.26%. Its scoring
  also removes prompt leakage and excludes high-CER cases. Therefore it
  motivates explicit pronunciation supervision, not a blanket claim that
  LoRA reduces CER. A Mongolian version needs validated pronunciation labels
  and training; adding unfamiliar phoneme spellings at inference is not a
  faithful reproduction. Our registered all-recording scoring remains unchanged.
- [PEFT-TTS](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/kwon25_interspeech.pdf)
  studies Korean adaptation of F5-TTS using text-conditioning, prompt and
  attention adapters. Its multi-speaker evaluation exposes a pronunciation/
  identity trade-off: increasing LoRA rank from 16 to 32 improves CER from
  7.220% to 5.358%, but SIM-O falls from 0.641 to 0.614 in that configuration.
  Prompt-adapter regularization matters, but its flow architecture and WavLM
  metric differ from our system. Increasing rank alone is not an evidence-based
  solution to our joint target.
- [ReStyle-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03632v1) separates text/reference
  guidance, combines style adapters with orthogonal weight updates, and weights
  supervised flow losses using generated speaker rewards. Its guidance branches
  are explicitly trained with condition dropout. We cannot assume equivalent
  unconditional branches in our autoregressive Qwen checkpoint. Orthogonal
  adapter fusion also needs meaningful distinct adapters; projecting our single
  language update against an arbitrary speaker basis would not reproduce it.

**Current decision:** complete the already registered codec-only routing test.
It changes reference-audio processing while checking earlier text states exactly,
but cached audio still affects subsequent generation. If it fails, inspect the
paired speech results before selecting another intervention. These papers do
not justify relaxing either group threshold, changing the scorer, removing
failed samples, or declaring internal cosine equivalent to perceived identity.

## Follow-up: absolute preference quality and regularization

Reviewed November 11, 2026 while the registered middle-attention run evaluates.

- [MPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00685v1) combines multidimensional preference
  selection with a cross-entropy term. Its unregularized model's CER worsens
  from 4.57% at 5k steps to 14.52% at 15k; regularization limits degradation.
  Chosen candidates must have zero CER in its selection procedure. This
  motivates testing an absolute-quality filter and a supervised anchor, but
  the Mandarin, single-codebook system differs from Qwen's 16-codebook model.
  Our short run has not exhibited its near-zero preference-loss collapse.
- [Does Expert Supervision Improve TTS?](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25292v1)
  studies real/expert targets for CosyVoice and GPT-SoVITS. Chosen likelihood
  can increase while intelligibility deteriorates, or decrease while it
  improves. Objective and beta sensitivity differ; bridge supervision does
  not consistently beat the base. Track fixed probes and actual synthesis,
  rather than diagnosing quality from preference loss or chosen likelihood
  alone. These are different targets from our generated-versus-generated pool.
- [NVIDIA's Magpie-TTS guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/tts/magpietts-po.html)
  recommends varied normal and challenging texts, multiple candidates, and
  illustrates filtering preferred outputs by absolute CER. Its example DPO
  configuration has zero SFT weight, so this does not establish that a
  supervised anchor is always necessary. Its online GRPO implementation is
  specific to Magpie and cannot be copied into Qwen unchanged.

**Local evidence and next decision:** only 137/357 current fitting pairs have
chosen samples meeting both screen thresholds, covering 29/48 fitting speakers.
Zero CER plus the voice threshold leaves 119 pairs across 26 speakers. An
absolute filter has a real diversity cost; first finish the registered run,
then compare a separately registered filtered/anchored objective if warranted.
Do not modify the running recipe or interpret visited-batch likelihood changes
as fixed-probe measurements. Every candidate still needs the unchanged speech
screen and, if it passes, the 114-recording confirmation.

**Measured follow-up:** the unfiltered middle-attention run and the subsequent
absolute-quality-filtered run both stopped after speech regressions. Filtering
alone did not meet the joint target; at update 4 it increased development CER
from 2.05% to 3.72% relative to the unfiltered run, while custom-reference
cosine stayed below 0.984. These outcomes do not support adopting the filter
as an established improvement.

A training-only gradient probe then found that a proposed mean-token CE weight
of 0.1 would contribute only 0.51% of the DPO gradient norm across 29 fitting
speakers. A separately registered comparison uses weight 5 (approximately 25%
on that initial probe), retaining the same filtered data and generation policy.
Its audited update 4 has development CER 4.10% and custom-reference cosine
0.97894; all six custom-reference recordings exactly match filtered update 4.
The objective change affects parameters but has not yet improved speech.
See [the calibrated comparison](0.6b-calibrated-quality-preferences.md) for
the actual checkpoint results and controls. This is a locally calibrated
experiment, not a reproduction of MPO or evidence that its weight is optimal.
