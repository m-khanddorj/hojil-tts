# Memory-separated codec-to-policy gradients

The direct speaker-gradient path now reaches the real 0.6B talker's existing
rank-4 middle-attention LoRA. This remains a read-only diagnostic, not a training
result or a passing speech checkpoint. The final goal remains CER below 3%
and original-reference cosine above 0.984 in both development/custom groups,
with all 114 confirmation recordings after a verified 26-recording screen.

## Objective and alignment

`training/codec_policy_replay.py` constructs the established full ICL sequence
with the frozen original speaker adapter. It extracts raw-model probabilities
for all 16 codebooks at each generated frame. The main-codebook probability is
conditional on an acoustic token; EOS is excluded from that acoustic softmax
but retained in the separately checked full sequence likelihood. Residual
probabilities condition on the recorded earlier codebooks. Sampling temperature
and top-k are not applied to these diagnostic probabilities.

Reference codec probabilities are constant one-hot tensors. The frozen codec
uses the recorded hard codes in its forward pass and the soft posterior in its
straight-through backward pass. Gradients are saved with respect to generated
probabilities only. A separate talker process recomputes the same probabilities,
checks agreement, and applies the saved vector-Jacobian product. No codec and
talker computation graphs need to coexist on the GPU.

Three tests on small instances of the actual Qwen talker and 16-codebook codec
pass. They compare the separate passes against a complete differentiable graph
at two trajectory lengths, verify the established likelihood including EOS,
and reject mismatched caches before backward. Frozen parameters have no
gradients. Historical registered modules were not edited.

## Native result

The three native processes completed successfully, sequentially. They restored
the audited unfiltered middle-attention update-4 checkpoint and used the same
preselected preference-fitting trajectory as the earlier native codec probe:
`balanced-114-draw-1`. This is an older-policy trajectory, explicitly off-policy;
no evaluation target was used.

| Stage | Result | Peak GPU allocation |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Collect actual posterior | Full likelihood exactly matches established implementation: -5105.680999755859 over 1,473 tokens | 3,764,584,448 bytes |
| Frozen codec/speaker gradient | Hard forward exactly matches native GPU audio; reference probabilities constant | 6,864,966,656 bytes |
| Policy replay | Maximum posterior discrepancy 0; finite nonzero gradients on all 32 LoRA tensors / 163,840 parameters | 3,937,047,552 bytes |

The policy gradient norm is 0.0055274289. Original acoustic weights, speaker
adapter and LoRA values remain unchanged; no optimizer step occurred. The
main acoustic probability mass averages 0.99942988, with minimum 0.95303524.
The conditional acoustic posterior must therefore not be presented as the
complete EOS-inclusive policy distribution.

Artifacts outside Git: `policy-codec-replay-probe/protocol.json`,

## What this does and does not establish

The two-stage gradient is correct for the tested local straight-through
surrogate. It is not an unbiased gradient of discrete autoregressive speech,
and a favorable internal speaker gradient does not establish better perceived
voice or pronunciation. No generated-speech improvement has been measured.

In particular, hard-forward speaker loss on a fixed recorded trajectory is
constant as policy parameters change. That value must not be used as a
validation trend or proof of optimization. A bounded training experiment needs
fresh policy-generated trajectories after updates, plus unchanged full speech
evaluation and a pronunciation constraint. Reference probabilities must remain
constant throughout. A stale-gradient multi-update run is not justified by
this diagnostic.

For initial data coverage, the audited pool contains 173 zero-ASR-CER fitting
recordings across 44 speakers. Restricting cosine to [0.96, 0.984) leaves 110
recordings across 36 speakers; 109 fit within the tested 172 combined reference
and generated frames. This is candidate training coverage, not a registered
selection or human pronunciation certification. Validation has 45 analogous
recordings across 15 speakers, of which 43 meet that frame bound. Training
memory bounds must not shorten or remove any final evaluation condition.

A separate [bounded fresh-rollout pilot](0.6b-direct-voice-pilot.md) is now
registered and launched with a tested pronunciation-anchor constraint and the
existing two-group speech/confirmation requirements. The read-only replay
evidence above remains unchanged and is not itself a training result.
